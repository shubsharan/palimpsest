# Research: Decipherment Headroom

## Decision

The minimum product question is whether semantic analysis can make coherent progress beyond a mechanical substitution attack without recognizing the source. The Amber observation answers yes.

Birch shows that recognizing a source can accelerate solving, but that observation is confounded by prior knowledge. Cobalt would test generalization to non-literary material; it does not change the binary product decision that semantic headroom exists.

## Claim Boundary

The accepted observation supports moving to revision dynamics on the retained unrecognized-literary profile. It does not support corpus-general, human-comparative, model-population, or publication-grade claims.

## Deferred Research

A later replication may add non-literary material, human comparison, multiple models, complete identification attacks, and immutable replay evidence if those claims become product-relevant.
